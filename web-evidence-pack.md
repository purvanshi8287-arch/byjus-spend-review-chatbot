# BYJU'S Spend Review — public web evidence pack

**Research cut-off: 26 October 2026.** This source map covers the BYJU'S spending-governance problem and proposal-review evidence needs. The project Evidence Register was not supplied, so these sources are not mapped to E01–E08 and are not assigned the project's verification statuses.

## Use and limitations

- Public sources establish historical facts, announcements, regulatory records and sector context. They cannot establish a new proposal's incremental revenue, ROI, demand, current internal budget or approval authority.
- Public FY22 figures have different scopes. Keep consolidated financials separate from standalone/core-business results.
- Historical losses and advertising expense support the project's governance-gap context; they do not prove any particular proposal caused those losses.
- Treat web sources as candidate external evidence until project/finance reviewers verify and record them in the Evidence Register.

## Proposal evidence that public web sources cannot provide

| Required evidence | Why it matters | Safe source/next step |
|---|---|---|
| Full proposal cost: fees, production, taxes, agency/activation, fulfilment, contingencies, payment milestones and contract duration | A headline fee is not the total commitment or its cash timing. | Approved proposal, draft contract and finance costing, shared only through an authorised internal channel. |
| Expected incremental benefit/revenue, contribution margin and attribution method | Gross revenue alone does not show contribution or causality. | Finance/marketing forecast with assumptions, scenario ranges, attribution window, counterfactual and accountable owner. |
| Campaign/partner/market cohort history: spend, reach, qualified leads, conversion, CAC, renewal/retention, incremental contribution | Tests prior precedent and campaign effectiveness. | Restricted finance/marketing systems; use only an authorised, aggregate and de-identified extract. |
| Subscription renewal, churn, purchase history, learning activity, feedback and complaint trends | The worksheet identifies CRM/subscription/LMS data as restricted; these measure retention and demand. | Authorised privacy-reviewed aggregates with population, definitions, period and missingness documented. No names, phone numbers, IDs, exact addresses or individual case notes. |
| Demand and price elasticity for the target segment | Market size or a competitor list does not show willingness to pay or incremental demand. | Approved primary survey/pilot or credible segment research with sample/methodology, checked against internal conversion/renewal data. |
| Comparable competitor prices and share for exact course, geography, format and period | Prices differ by exam, course duration, teacher, modality, discounts and access. Public pages are snapshots. | Specify the market; collect dated like-for-like offers and an approved market-share source. No universal “edtech average” is established here. |
| Expansion market-entry demand, regulations/tax, operating cost, partner/centre availability and FX exposure | These depend on the proposed location, date and model; no location was specified. | Identify the geography, then use local regulators, official statistics and validated internal entry-cost plans. |
| Customer-trust/regulatory clearance for the actual campaign | General news or one regulator proceeding does not decide whether a particular claim or endorsement is compliant or trusted. | Current legal/compliance review of final creative, claims, disclosures, audience and data use; representative research if needed. |
| Internal approval threshold, budget availability, existing commitments and policy | Public sources do not reveal current delegated authority, available budget or commitments. | Obtain current approved policy and finance records from the designated owner. Never invent a threshold. |
| Evidence distinguishing spend effects from external demand/funding effects | Correlation cannot isolate why financial performance changed. | Internal segment/cohort time series joined to campaign/customer outcomes, compared with defined external market periods using an approved method. |

## Keep these claims out until verified

1. **30%/40% trust and decision claims:** a public small-sample paper reports similar percentages, but the supplied team files do not cite it or supply the survey dataset. Do not state these as a representative BYJU'S customer survey without the original source, population, questionnaire, date and provenance.
2. **FEMA amount as a final violation finding:** a 2023 report quotes ED's ₹9,362.35 crore show-cause allegations and records BYJU'S denial that it received the notice. That is not by itself a final adjudicated penalty or current legal status. See [New Indian Express, 22 Nov 2023](https://www.newindianexpress.com/business/2023/Nov/22/ed-notice-to-byjus-for-rs-93k-crore-fema-violations-2635110.html). Re-check official case records before current legal use.
3. **Industry benchmark:** no comparable approved benchmark is supplied. BYJU'S own historical advertising total is not an industry norm.
4. **Causation:** financial statements, sponsorship announcements, regulatory proceedings and sector funding trends do not prove any one expense caused later distress.
5. **Synthetic examples:** useful only to illustrate a method, clearly labelled fictional; never evidence of benefit, profitability or approval.

## Pre-commitment evidence gate

1. Capture proposal type, objective, amount, total cost, timing, geography, customer/partner and decision owner.
2. Quantify expected benefit where possible; show source, assumptions, period and attribution/counterfactual.
3. Compare only with relevant approved precedent and state limitations; do not add unsupported ROI.
4. Check demand, customer trust, regulatory and data-governance evidence; mark unknowns clearly.
5. Date and attribute external sources; keep them as candidate evidence until formally approved.
6. Show source conflicts, assumptions, gaps and human-review triggers.
7. Authorised finance and business leadership decide. The assistant does not approve, reject, sign, negotiate or commit funds.
